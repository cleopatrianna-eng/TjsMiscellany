Curated Objects

The Emporium

Things worth owning — beautiful, useful, and lasting

Everything here is something I have bought, used, and can honestly recommend. No sponsored placements. No algorithmic suggestions. Only things that have earned a permanent place on my desk, shelf, or in my daily life.

Stationery
Favourite

Writing Instruments

Pilot Custom 74 Fountain Pen

The pen I reach for every day, without exception. The fine nib is precisely calibrated; the balance in the hand is near perfect. Japanese engineering at its most considered.

£95 — £120

New Find

Notebooks

Leuchtturm1917 A5 Dotted

I have tried many notebooks. This is the one I always return to. The paper takes ink beautifully without feathering; the dot grid is unobtrusive enough to forget it's there.

£18 — £22

Ink

Diamine Oxblood Fountain Pen Ink

A deep burgundy-brown with wonderful character — it reads as dark in dim light and reveals its redness in sunlight. Ideal for correspondence that should look as though it was written with intention.

£8

Pencils

Blackwing 602 Pencils

The pencil of Steinbeck and Nabokov and a great many other writers who cared about such things. The semi-firm lead is perfect for prose drafts; the eraser is unmatched. A luxury with a case.

£22 for 12

Books & Editions
Favourite

Fine Editions

Folio Society Selected Editions

I buy Folio editions selectively, for books I expect to read more than once. The physical quality — paper, binding, illustration — is consistently superb. Their Middlemarch and Collected Poems of Keats are particularly fine.

£30 — £100+

New

Art Books

The Art of the Book: Illuminated Manuscripts

Published by Thames & Hudson, this survey of medieval manuscript illumination is itself a beautiful object — large format, excellent colour reproduction, and scholarship that never loses sight of the images.

£55

Poetry

Faber Poetry Classics Series

The Faber clothbound poets — Larkin, Hughes, Plath, Heaney — are beautifully produced at a sensible price. These are books you can carry without anxiety and lend without regret.

£14 — £18

Objects
Favourite

Lighting

Anglepoise Original 1227 Lamp

British design at its most enduring. I have had mine for eleven years and it has not ceased to be either beautiful or functional. The Forest Green version sits on my desk and I consider it daily.

£195

Book Care

Cast Iron Botanical Bookends

Found at a Gloucestershire antiques fair; reproduced by a small foundry in Birmingham. Heavy enough to hold a full shelf of hardbacks; beautiful enough to leave out as objects in their own right.

£65 per pair

New Find

Ceramic

Studio Pottery Mug (Various Makers)

I have stopped drinking tea from anything that isn't a handmade mug. The difference in the experience is not imaginary. Small British potters whose work I admire: Micki Schloessingk, Sarah Flynn, James and Tilla Waters.

£25 — £75

Paper Goods

G. F. Smith Colorplan Writing Paper

The finest writing paper available without going to a stationer's specialist. The Pale Gold and Smoke Blue colourways are particularly handsome. Takes fountain pen ink without bleed; envelopes are correspondingly good.

£12 — £25 per pack

Paper Goods

Wax Seal Set with Custom Stamp

An unambiguous extravagance that I do not regret in the slightest. The seal goes on every letter I send. The wax (Forest Green or Deep Burgundy) arrives in a state that communicates, before it is even opened, that someone cared.

£35 — £85
